## 第 3 页 — Result 1: the tokenizer explains most of it（约 2 分钟）

**这页一句话：** 结果一：切法解释了约 60% 的差别；同样的留出集损失下，6 字母背得多得多。

**English**

Here is the first result.

On the left: I trained 18 models, three tokenizers times three objectives times two seeds. Then I asked where the variance in canary memorization comes from. About 60 percent comes from the tokenizer. About 26 percent comes from the objective. The seed is under one percent.

On the right: I wanted to know if coarse tokens are only faster. So I compare the models at the same held-out loss, not at the same epoch. At a held-out loss of 2.2 bits, the 6-mer model has memorized about 70 percent of a canary it saw once. The 3-mer model has 24 percent, and the 1-mer model 11 percent.

So coarser tokens memorize sooner, and also more. Early stopping does not remove the gap.

I'd be curious whether this matches your intuition from the four models.

**中文**

这是第一个结果。

左边：我训练了 18 个模型，三种切法乘三种训练目标乘两个种子。然后看卧底记忆量的差别从哪儿来。约 60% 来自切法，约 26% 来自训练目标，种子不到 1%。

右边：我想知道粗切法是不是只是更快。所以我在同样的留出集损失下比较模型，而不是在同样的轮数下比较。留出集损失 2.2 bit 时，6 字母模型把只见过一次的卧底背下了约 70%，3 字母是 24%，1 字母是 11%。

所以粗切法背得更快，也更多。提前停止消除不了这个差距。

我很想知道，这和你从那四个模型得到的直觉是否一致。

## 第 4 页 — Result 2: not sequence length, not parameter count（约 2 分钟）

**这页一句话：** 结果二：不是序列长度，不是参数量；数据超过容量后只有 6 字母还能抽出卧底。

**English**

The next question was why. I tested two common explanations.

The first is sequence length. With 6-mers the sequence is six times shorter. This is the conjecture in Kharitonov and colleagues, 2021. I padded the 3-mer and 6-mer sequences to the same length as the 1-mer sequence. Memorization barely changed.

The second is parameter count, because a 6-mer vocabulary adds about two million embedding parameters. I shrank the body so the total is the same. Again, no change.

What is left is the unit of prediction: how many nucleotides one softmax predicts at a time.

One more result, in the bottom right. With twenty times more data, above the model's capacity, the 1-mer and 3-mer models extract none of the repeated canaries. The 6-mer model extracts all of them. DNABERT and Nucleotide Transformer both use 6-mers, so I think this matters for real models.

**中文**

下一个问题是"为什么"。我检验了两个常见的解释。

第一个是序列长度。用 6 字母切，序列短了六倍。这是 Kharitonov 等人 2021 年的猜想。我把 3 字母和 6 字母的序列补到和 1 字母一样长，记忆量几乎没变。

第二个是参数量，因为 6 字母词表多出约 200 万嵌入参数。我把模型主体缩小，让总参数一样。结果还是没变。

剩下的就是预测单位：一个 softmax 一次预测几个字母。

右下角还有一个结果。数据加到 20 倍、超过模型容量后，1 字母和 3 字母模型一个重复的卧底都抽不出来，6 字母模型全部抽得出来。DNABERT 和 Nucleotide Transformer 都用 6 字母，所以我觉得这对真实模型有意义。

## 第 5 页 — Result 3: canary position and positional encoding（约 2 分钟）

**这页一句话：** 结果三：我一开始犯的错——卧底位置固定，模型学的是位置；换成相对位置编码后粗切法又能认出来。

**English**

This one started as a mistake of mine.

In my first runs, every copy of a canary sat at the same offset in its training sequence. Then I tested this: take a canary planted 16 times, put it into a brand-new host sequence, and ask if the model still ranks it first.

The grey bars are the fixed offset. The model recognizes it every time. The red bars are random offsets. Recognition almost disappears: 0, 5 and 32 percent.

So the model had learned what comes at that position. It had not learned the sequence. Training three times longer did not help.

Then I replaced the absolute position embedding with relative positions, RoPE. Those are the orange bars. For 6-mer, recognition goes back up to 80 percent. For 1-mer it stays at zero.

So positional encoding seems to be the part of the architecture that really matters here. And real GLMs differ in it.

The sample is small, 20 canaries and 2 seeds per bar, so please read these as rough numbers.

**中文**

这一条最初是我自己犯的错。

我最早的实验里，卧底的每一份拷贝都放在训练序列的同一个位置。后来我做了这个检验：拿一个塞了 16 遍的卧底，放进一段全新的序列里，看模型还会不会把它排在第一。

灰色柱子是位置固定的情况，模型每次都认得出来。红色柱子是位置随机，认出率几乎没了：0、5、32%。

也就是说，模型学到的是那个位置上该出现什么，而不是序列本身。训练时间加到三倍也没用。

然后我把绝对位置编码换成了相对位置编码 RoPE，就是橙色柱子。6 字母的认出率回到了 80%，1 字母还是 0。

所以位置编码看起来是架构里真正起作用的那一部分，而真实的 GLM 恰好在这一点上各不相同。

样本很小，每根柱子是 20 个卧底、2 个种子，所以请把这些当成粗略的数字。
